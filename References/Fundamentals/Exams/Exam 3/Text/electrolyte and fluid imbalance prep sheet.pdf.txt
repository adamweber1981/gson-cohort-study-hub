Electrolytes & Fluid Balance
Read this before class — it will make the lecture click faster.



  Big picture: Electrolytes keep nerves firing, hearts beating, and muscles contracting. Small shifts
  outside the normal range can be life-threatening. Today covers sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium,
  and fluid volume — know your normals, know your signs, know your interventions.



  POTASSIUM — MOST DANGEROUS IMBALANCE


    Hypokalemia <3.5 — LOW
    Causes: loop/thiazide diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, NG suction, excessive alcohol, chronic kidney
    disease
    S&S: muscle weakness, respiratory paralysis, cardiac arrhythmias, ↓reflexes, flat/inverted T wave
    Tx: cardiac monitor; oral K⁺ foods; IV K⁺ if critically low — NEVER IV push; MAX 20 mEq/hr

    Hyperkalemia >5.0 — HIGH
    Causes: renal failure, K⁺-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs, sepsis, excessive intake
    S&S: muscle weakness, chest pain, cardiac dysrhythmias, peaked T waves, cardiac arrest
    Tx: cardiac monitor; calcium gluconate (stabilizes heart); IV insulin (shifts K⁺ into cells); kayexalate;
    dialysis if renal failure



  SODIUM


    Hyponatremia <135 — LOW
    Causes: excess water, SIADH, heart/kidney/liver failure, diuretics, vomiting
    S&S: nausea, confusion, headache, restlessness, seizures (brain cells swell)
    Tx: fluid restriction; antiemetics/antidiarrheals; avoid gum and hidden fluid sources; correct slowly

    Hypernatremia >145 — HIGH
    Causes: DI, dehydration, fever, vomiting, gastroenteritis, impaired thirst, CKD
    S&S: confusion, thirst, ↓urine, ↓LOC, seizures (brain cells shrink)
    Tx: oral fluids if mild; hypotonic IV fluids; decrease sodium slowly — rapid correction causes
    cerebral edema
CALCIUM


 Hypocalcemia <8.4 — LOW
 Causes: ↓Vit D, hypoparathyroidism, renal disease, hormonal changes
 S&S: numbness/tingling, muscle cramps, tetany, seizures, + Chvostek's & Trousseau's, chest pain,
 dysrhythmias
 Tx: seizure precautions; oral Ca²⁺ + Vit D; IV calcium gluconate if severe; correct hypomagnesemia
 concurrently

 Hypercalcemia >10.5 — HIGH
 Causes: hyperparathyroidism, cancer, prolonged bed rest, Vit D toxicity
 S&S: GI symptoms first (N/V, abd pain), confusion, thirst, bone pain, muscle weakness
 Tx: IV NS + loop diuretics; encourage ambulation; fall precautions; seizure precautions if severe



MAGNESIUM


 Hypomagnesemia <1.5 — LOW
 Causes: loop/thiazide diuretics, PPIs, malnutrition, diarrhea, alcoholism, decreased intestinal
 absorption
 S&S: mirrors hypocalcemia — N/V, fatigue, tetany, dysrhythmias, + Chvostek's & Trousseau's
 Tx: oral Mg²⁺; IV magnesium sulfate if severe — high-alert medication, double-check dose;
 monitor respiratory rate; correct hypokalemia/hypocalcemia concurrently

 Hypermagnesemia >2.5 — HIGH
 Causes: renal failure, antacids/laxatives with Mg²⁺, trauma, hypothyroidism
 S&S: ↓DTRs (early warning), hypotension, flushing, headache, blurred vision, respiratory depression,
 cardiac arrest
 Tx: IV calcium gluconate (antidote); loop diuretics; IV fluids; cardiac monitor; dialysis if severe

• Key link: low magnesium → cannot correct low calcium or low potassium until magnesium is replaced
 first
• Loss of DTRs = early magnesium toxicity warning — notify provider immediately before giving
 more magnesium



FLUID VOLUME


 Deficit (Hypovolemia)
 Causes: vomiting, diarrhea, bleeding, burns, sweating, polyuria, fever, trauma
 S&S: ↑HR, ↓BP, dry membranes, poor skin turgor, ↑urine specific gravity, ↑BUN/Hgb/Hct
 Tx: treat cause; oral rehydration if mild; IV fluids if moderate/severe; hypovolemic shock at >20%
 fluid loss

 Excess (Hypervolemia)
 Causes: heart failure, kidney/liver failure, nephrotic syndrome, excessive IV fluids
 S&S: ↑BP, bounding pulse, crackles, JVD, pitting edema, weight gain, ↓BUN/Hgb/Hct
 Tx: treat cause; diuretics; fluid and sodium restriction; daily weights; dialysis if renal failure
