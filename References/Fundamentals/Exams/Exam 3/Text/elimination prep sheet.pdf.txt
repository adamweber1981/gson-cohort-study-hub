Elimination
Read this before class — it will make the lecture click faster.



  Big picture: Today covers both urinary and bowel elimination — assessment terms, common
  alterations, devices and interventions, and key conditions. Elimination is deeply personal; nurses
  prioritize privacy, dignity, and early identification of problems.



  URINARY ASSESSMENT TERMS

   Urgency — immediate desire to void

   Dysuria — painful urination

   Frequency — voiding >8×/day

   Polyuria — excessive urination

   Oliguria — diminished urination

   Nocturia — urination at night

   Hematuria — blood in urine

   Anuria — no urine output


  URINARY ALTERATIONS & DEVICES

  • Incontinence: involuntary passage of urine; interventions — pelvic floor exercises, medications, limit
     caffeine/alcohol, skin protection
  • Retention: bladder cannot fully empty; signs — inability to void, distention, hesitancy, weak stream;
     intervene based on cause
  • Bladder scan: ultrasound to estimate post-void residual; reduces unnecessary catheterizations

  • External catheters: condom catheter (male), Purewick (female); continuous suction 40–65 mmHg

  • Indwelling catheter: sterile procedure; requires order; drain bag below bladder; empty when half
     full; assess for removal daily



  BOWEL ALTERATIONS

  • Constipation: <3 BM/week; hard dry stool; interventions — fluids, high fiber, stool softeners,
     suppositories, enemas, exercise
  • Impaction: stool stuck in rectum from unrelieved constipation; may have liquid stool around the
     blockage; treat with enema or manual disimpaction
  • Obstruction: partial or complete blockage — can be life-threatening; N/V, distention, severe
     constipation; may require NG tube or surgery
  • Diarrhea: frequent loose/watery stool; monitor hydration; avoid irritants; Imodium

  • Ileus: temporary loss of peristalsis; absent bowel sounds; NPO; common after abdominal surgery



  C. DIFF

  • Bacterial colon infection; risk factors: antibiotic use, age >65, weakened immune system, recent
     hospitalization
  • Signs: watery, foul-smelling, yellow/mucous diarrhea; abdominal cramps; fever; rapid HR

  • Complications: dehydration → kidney failure; sepsis

  • Contact precautions; antibiotics (vancomycin or Flagyl); IV fluids; stool culture to confirm

  • Soap and water only for hand hygiene — alcohol-based sanitizer does NOT kill C. diff spores




  OSTOMIES

  • Ileostomy: small intestine diverted through stoma; stool is liquid

  • Colostomy: colon diverted; stool consistency depends on location (ascending = liquid; sigmoid =
     formed)
  • Stoma assessment: should be moist, pink or red; blue = poor perfusion (call provider); black = tissue
     death (likely needs OR)
  • Assess surrounding skin for breakdown with every bag change
