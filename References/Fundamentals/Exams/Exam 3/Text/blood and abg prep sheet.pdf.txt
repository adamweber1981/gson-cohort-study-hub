Blood Transfusions & ABGs
Read this before class — it will make the lecture click faster.



  Big picture: Two distinct topics covered today — blood transfusion safety and acid-base interpretation.
  Both require pattern recognition and systematic thinking. ABGs are read the same way every time;
  learning the steps now makes it automatic.



  BLOOD PRODUCTS

  • Packed RBCs: restore blood levels without substantially increasing volume

  • Whole blood: used for significant blood loss (trauma, surgery)

  • Plasma: liquid portion containing clotting factors

  • Platelets: stop or prevent bleeding; used in transplants, cancer, surgery

  • Universal donor: O− · Universal recipient: AB+

  • Blood type and crossmatch required before transfusion



  BEFORE & DURING TRANSFUSION

  • Signed consent; pre-transfusion vital signs

  • Two-nurse check before beginning

  • IV size ideally 18–20 gauge; infuse with normal saline only

  • RN stays with client for first 15 minutes — most common time for reaction

  • Vital signs at 15 min after start, then minimum hourly

  • Must complete within 4 hours of leaving blood bank

  • If reaction: stop transfusion immediately, keep IV open with NS, remain with client, send blood bag
     and tubing to blood bank



  ABG NORMAL VALUES & INTERPRETATION STEPS


                                                            pH
                                                        7.35 – 7.45
                                                     <7.35 = acidosis
                                                     >7.45 = alkalosis


                                                           CO2
                                                      35 – 45 mmHg
                                                  Respiratory component


                                                           HCO3
                                                      22 – 26 mEq/L
                                                   Metabolic component


    1 Look at pH — acidosis (<7.35) or alkalosis (>7.45)?


    2 Use ROME — Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal. If pH and CO2 move in opposite directions →
       respiratory. If pH and HCO3 move in same direction → metabolic.

    3 Identify compensation — if all three values are abnormal → partially compensated. If pH is back
       in normal range → fully compensated.
THE FOUR ABG DISORDERS


 Respiratory Acidosis — pH ↓ · CO2 ↑
 Lungs retaining too much CO2
 Causes (DEPRESS): Drugs, Edema, Pneumonia, Respiratory center damage, Emboli, Spasm (asthma),
 Sac elasticity (COPD)
 S&S: shallow respirations, confusion, fatigue, headache, cyanosis
 Tx: improve ventilation; O2; bronchodilators; naloxone if opioid-related; may need intubation if CO2
 >50

 Respiratory Alkalosis — pH ↑ · CO2 ↓
 Lungs losing too much CO2
 Causes (TACHypnea): Temperature elevation, Anxiety, Conditions causing ↓O2,
 Hypoxemia/hyperventilation
 S&S: rapid breathing, tingling in extremities, light-headedness, tachycardia
 Tx: treat cause; keep calm; coach slow breathing; fall precautions

 Metabolic Acidosis — pH ↓ · HCO3 ↓
 Kidneys have too little bicarb / too much H+
 Causes (MUDPILES): Malnutrition, Uremia, DKA, Poisoning, Isoniazid, Lactic acidosis, Ethanol, Severe
 diarrhea
 S&S: Kussmaul respirations, fruity breath (DKA), confusion, flushed skin
 Tx: treat cause; IV bicarb if severe; seizure precautions; monitor K⁺ closely

 Metabolic Alkalosis — pH ↑ · HCO3 ↑
 Kidneys have too much bicarb / too little H+
 Causes: excess antacids, diuretics, vomiting, prolonged NG suction, hyperaldosteronism, blood
 transfusions
 S&S: hypoventilation, tingling, muscle twitching, confusion
 Tx: treat cause; IV fluids; replace K⁺; antiemetics; monitor for respiratory distress



COMPENSATION — HOW THE BODY RESPONDS

• Lungs respond first — fast (minutes to hours); control CO2; if acidotic → blow off CO2; if alkalotic →

 retain CO2
• Kidneys respond second — slow (hours to days); control H+ and HCO3; if acidotic → reabsorb HCO3

 and secrete H+; if alkalotic → reabsorb H+ and secrete HCO3
• When one organ causes the problem, the other compensates — this is why all three values may be
 abnormal in partially or fully compensated states
