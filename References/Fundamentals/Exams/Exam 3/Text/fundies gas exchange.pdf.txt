                                   Hyperventilation

       • Increase in respiratory rate
       • Increased in carbon dioxide (CO2)
         exhaled
           • Lower levels of CO2 in blood
       • Causes:
           • Anxiety attack
           • Infection such as pneumonia
           • Lung disease
               • COPD
               • Asthma
           • Diabetic ketoacidosis
           • Brain injury
           • Drug use

                                                                                                              3
                              Copyright 2022 Assessment Technologies Institute, L.L.C. All rights reserved.




 During hyperventilation, an increased amount of carbon dioxide is
exhaled, leading to lower than expected levels of carbon dioxide in the
blood. These low levels of carbon dioxide can cause the client’s arterial pH
to increase and alkalosis to develop.

                         Hyperventilation cont.

Manifestations

• Increased respiratory rate (>20)
• Weakness
• Dizziness
• Headache
• Anxiety
• Increased heart rate
• Difficulty breathing
• Numbness/tingling in fingers

Interventions

• Breathing techniques
• Medication
• Airway management

                                  Hypoventilation

       • Decrease in respiratory rate
       • Increased in CO2 in blood
       • Causes:
           • Neuromuscular disorders
               • Muscular dystrophy
           • Medications
               • Barbiturates
               • Narcotics
               • Benzodiazepines
           • Neurologic disorders
           • Trauma
           • Lung diseases
               • COPD
               • Asthma

                                                                                                            5
                            Copyright 2022 Assessment Technologies Institute, L.L.C. All rights reserved.




Hypoventilation is shallow breathing with a lower than expected
respiratory rate. It leads to too much CO2 in the blood and not enough
oxygen. Elevated CO2 levels increase the amount of carbonic acid in the
blood. This decreases the pH of the blood and results in acidosis.

                     Hypoventilation cont.

• Decreased respiratory rate (<12)
• Anxiety
• Weakness
• Disturbed sleep
• Change in level of consciousness
• Dysrhythmias

Interventions

• Breathing techniques
• Medication
• Airway management

    • Considered a medication

Supplemental oxygen may be required for clients with cardiopulmonary
issues to achieve a fraction of inspired oxygen greater than 21%—the
percentage of oxygen in room air. This therapy is used to treat
hypoxemia.

CPAP
• Continuous positive airway
  pressure
• Delivers flow through mask or
  nosepiece
• Constant, steady flow to keep
  alveoli open and improve oxygen
  in blood
• Supplemental oxygen can be
  added to system
• Used primarily for sleep apnea

BiPAP
• Bilevel positive airway pressure
• Higher pressures when inhaling
• Lower pressure when exhaling
• Can use if airway collapses during
  sleep
• Used for clients with disorders that
  cause muscle weakness inhibiting
  breathing
   • COPD
   • Heart failure

        Interventions to Improve Oxygenation
    • Sputum Collection
       • Assists in diagnosis and treatment
       • Collect before eating or drinking
       • Try to collect 1-2tsp in sterile cup
       • May need chest physiotherapy to loosen secretions
    • Chest Physiotherapy
       • Percussion of chest with vibration
       • Helps loosen thick secretions
       • Lasts 20-30 minutes
    • Incentive Spirometer
       • Used to promote deep breathing
       • Provides feedback on inspiratory volume
       • Educate on proper use
       • Help prevent atelectasis

                               Copyright 2022 Assessment Technologies Institute, L.L.C. All rights reserved.




Nurses should educate clients on the purpose of the IS and proper use of
the device, and should ensure that the clients perform a return
demonstration. When using the IS, clients should understand that the
device is to be used 10 repetitions per hour with each breath held for 3 to
5 seconds. Nurses should assist clients in setting a target goal and explain
the importance of using the IS. With continued use, pulmonary function
will improve and breath sounds will become clear.

• Purse-lipped breathing
   • Technique to help with breathlessness
   • Can help release air trapped
   • Promotes CO2 elimination
   • Relax neck and shoulders and breath in through
     nostrils and out through pursed lips
• Coughing & deep breathing
   • Prevent atelectasis and pneumonia
   • Encourage regular use
   • Use pillow to splint abdomen and chest

COPD and COPD Exacerbation
  • Characterized by persistent
    respiratory symptoms and airflow
    limitations
  • Pathologic changes in airways
  • Risk factors:
     • Tobacco use
     • Environmental and occupational
       exposure to dust/fumes
     • Respiratory infections
     • Extreme temperatures
     • Treatment nonadherence
