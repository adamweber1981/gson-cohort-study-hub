Upper Respiratory Medications
Antihistamines
Decrease allergy symptoms by blocking histamine
First generation: Diphenhydramine (sedating)
Second generation: Cetirizine (less sedating)
Antitussives
Suppress the cough reflex
Opioid: Codeine
Non opioid: Dextromethorphan

SLIDE 4
Lower Respiratory Medications
Bronchoconstriction: Airways become narrow, making it difficult for air to move in and out.
  Medications
Albuterol (SABA)
Salmeterol (LABA)
Ipratropium (SAMA)
Tiotropium (LAMA)
Airway Inflammation: Inflammation causes swelling of the airways and increased mucus production
    Medications
Fluticasone
Montelukast
Excess Mucus and Air Trapping: Can contribute to coughing, wheezing, and shortness of breath.
    Medications
Ipratropium/Tiotropium (decrease secretions)

SLIDE 6
Inhaled corticosteroids and leukotriene modifiers
Fluticasone
Reduce inflammation
Often combined with LABA
Rinse mouth and spit after use
Do not stop abruptly
Montelukast
Blocks leukotriene receptors
Improved airflow, reduce mucus
Not for acute exacerbations
Assess for mood and behavior changes
